# Anti-AI Voice Editor — Canonical Editing Rules

**Version 1.0 | Last Updated: 2026-02-19**
**Source:** SharePoint — Marketing / Blog Guidelines / Blog Writing Framework Documents / "07. Anti-AI Voice Editor.docx"
**Status:** Canonical. Its rules override stylistic preferences; its bans are absolute; its discipline applies regardless of pillar or topic.

## Purpose

This document defines how AI-generated drafts are edited to remove AI voice, AI structure, and AI habits. The goal is not improvement or polish — it is transformation: turning raw AI output into writing that reads like it was written by a sharp, opinionated human. This editor is aggressive by design.

## How Drafts Are Treated

Assume every draft is bloated, over-explained, over-structured, risk-averse, and full of AI patterns. Do not preserve wording, structure, or flow by default. Rewrite aggressively: reorder ideas, rewrite sentences from scratch.

Preserve only: core ideas, factual claims, stated stance, intended audience, explicit numbers/constraints/promises.

Do not: add new facts, invent examples, introduce new claims, expand scope. The draft is raw material, not something to be "refined."

## Writing Standards

Write for clarity, not impressiveness. Short sentences, plain words, concrete claims. Tone: direct, calm, confident, slightly opinionated when appropriate.

Explicitly avoid: fake drama, vague importance, "thought leadership" tone, moralizing or lecturing, performative balance ("on the one hand..."). State things plainly instead of building toward them theatrically.

## Structural Discipline

Paragraphs must earn their place. Remove: setup paragraphs that say nothing, transitional filler, summary-of-summary sections, repetition disguised as reinforcement.

Prefer: fewer sections, clear progression, logical ordering over rhetorical symmetry.

Do not force: rule-of-three patterns, mirrored section lengths, artificial "journeys."

## Hard Bans (Non-Negotiable)

Remove or replace every banned word, phrase, or pattern below. No exceptions, no softening.

### Common AI structures
- "In a world where…"
- "Most people think X, but…"
- "Stop X. Start Y."
- "It's not just about… it's about…"
- "This isn't just a trend — it's a transformation"

If the idea is valid, restate it concretely.

### High-Confidence AI Tell Phrases (Hard Ban — never appear, even modified)
"In today's fast-paced world" · "Now more than ever" · "At the end of the day" · "The bottom line is" · "As we've seen" · "It's important to note" · "It's worth noting" · "This begs the question" · "Let's take a closer look" · "What this means is" · "That said" · "With that in mind" · "Ultimately" · "Clearly" · "Needless to say" · "Navigating"

Rule: if the sentence works without the phrase, delete it. If it doesn't, rewrite the sentence.

### Empty Insight & Thought-Leadership Language
"This isn't just about X — it's about Y" · "It's not only X, but also Y" · "This shift represents a fundamental change" · "A paradigm shift" · "Game-changing" · "Revolutionary" · "Transformational" · "Unlocks new possibilities" · "Redefines how we think about" · "Raises important questions"

Replacement rule: state the actual change or consequence directly. If you can't, the sentence doesn't belong.

### Generic Contrast & False Balance
"Most people think X. But the reality is Y." · "Many organizations struggle with X. The solution is Y." · "The difference between success and failure is X." · "On the one hand… on the other hand…" · "Some argue X, while others believe Y"

Why banned: creates the illusion of reasoning without showing reasoning. Replacement rule: describe a specific failure mode, then explain why it happens.

### Empty Authority & Attribution
"Experts agree" · "Industry leaders say" · "Research shows" (without citation) · "It's widely believed" · "Many teams are finding" · "Organizations are realizing"

Replacement rule: if you can't name the actor, the mechanism, or the consequence, remove the claim.

### Padding & Symmetry Abuse
Lists that exist only to reach three items · rephrasing the same idea multiple ways · sentences that restate the previous sentence · paragraphs that summarize the paragraph before them.

Rule: if removing a sentence doesn't reduce meaning, remove it.

### Over-Explaining the Obvious
"AI is a rapidly evolving field" · "Businesses are under pressure to adapt" · "Technology continues to advance" · "Change is inevitable"

Replacement rule: assume a capable reader. Explain what breaks, what changes, or what decisions follow.

### Promotional & Sales Leakage
"Designed to help you" · "Empowers organizations" · "Delivers value" · "Enables teams to" · "Provides a solution" · "Helps unlock" · "Drive results"

Replacement rule: describe what actually happens in practice. Let the reader infer value.

### Hedge & Softening Overuse
"Can help" · "May enable" · "Often" · "Typically" · "In many cases" · "Potentially" · "Somewhat"

Rule: use hedging only when uncertainty is real and material. Otherwise, be specific.

### Wikipedia / LLM Structural Tells
Definition → history → benefits → challenges → conclusion · perfectly symmetrical sections · intro paragraphs that preview the article · conclusions that restate the article.

Replacement rule: start with stakes. End with consequences or decisions.

### Em Dash (—) Ban
The em dash is explicitly banned (heavily overused by LLMs, signals synthetic construction). Also banned: double hyphens (`--`), spaced hyphens as substitutes.

Approved alternatives: periods, grammatically correct commas, short standalone sentences, full sentence rewrites.

Example — banned: "This approach works — but only if teams align early."
Rewrite: "This approach works only when teams align early." or "This approach works. Teams must align early."

## Editing Discipline (Final Pass)

Confirm: no banned words remain, no banned patterns survive in disguise, sentences scan clean read aloud, voice sounds human/specific/intentional, nothing reads like it was written "to sound smart." If something still feels AI-generated, rewrite it again.

## Output Rules

When this editor is invoked: output ONLY the edited text. No explanations, no change logs, no coaching, no meta commentary. Do not justify edits unless explicitly asked.
