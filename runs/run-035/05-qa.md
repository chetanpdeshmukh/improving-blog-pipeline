### WARN Items (Recommended Fixes)

**1. Target audience unnamed (Checkpoint 1B)**
> The article addresses enterprise leaders implicitly. One sentence in the intro — "If you're a CTO, VP of Engineering, or an executive funding an AI initiative…" — would sharpen targeting and improve click qualification from search.

**2. Transcript grounding unverifiable (Checkpoint 2A)**
> No transcript was provided for this review. All claims and examples were evaluated on internal plausibility. If the transcript contradicts any example or reframes the oil and gas story, the article needs to reflect that.

**3. IFT and oil and gas examples lack outcomes (Checkpoint 2C)**
> "The result was reduced site-selection risk on drilling decisions" — no metric, no timeframe, no before/after. Same with IFT: the architectural decision is described but no deployment outcome is stated. Even a directional claim ("within six months, IFT's researchers were querying 80 years of literature without touching a general search engine") would close the gap.

**4. No Skeptic's View; "Half the Team" section presents no downsides (Checkpoint 4B)**
> The article never acknowledges that AI-driven team compression creates real organizational risk: morale damage, knowledge concentration, attrition of experienced contributors. One sentence in that section acknowledging the transition risk would add credibility and prevent the section from reading as unqualified advocacy.

**5. IFT and oil and gas war stories need narrative arc (Checkpoint 4C)**
> Both examples state a decision and a descriptor of outcome but skip the tension. What was the pressure that forced the decision? What was the risk of the alternative? Even one additional sentence per example showing the decision point would move these from descriptive to narrative.

**6. Generic close on the data moat section (Checkpoint 4E)**
> "The companies discovering this fastest are the ones whose data infrastructure decisions from five years ago now look like competitive strategy." This sentence could appear unchanged in a Gartner report. Anchor it to a specific technology era, decision type, or industry pattern from the SME's experience.

**7. Section depth is uniform when it should not be (Checkpoint 4F)**
> Six sections, all three paragraphs. The values section and the exit-speed section carry the most conceptual weight in the article. Both could support one additional paragraph without becoming bloated. The 30-second call section, by contrast, is doing closing work and earns its brevity.

**8. No question-format H2s (Checkpoint 5A / 5B)**
> None of the six H2s are questions. Reworking two to three of them into search-query-matching questions ("How Should You Evaluate an AI Consulting Firm?", "What Is the Real Security Risk When You Deploy Copilot?") would add snippet eligibility without changing the article's tone.

**9. No internal link to Improving.com (Checkpoint 5D)**
> At minimum, the community of practice mention in "Measure the Exit Speed" is a natural anchor for an internal link to an Improving services or about page.

**10. No structured elements (Checkpoint 5E)**
> The article is solid prose but has no tables, numbered frameworks, or decision checklists. A simple two-column table in the "Half the Team" section — "Old frame vs. Correct frame" — would create a citable, AI-indexable element and take 30 seconds to add.

**11. Word count at the floor (Checkpoint 4A)**
> ~815 words passes the 800-word minimum but by very little. Expanding two war stories (IFT and oil and gas) with one narrative sentence each, and adding the structured element above, would push the count to a more comfortable 900–950 without padding.
