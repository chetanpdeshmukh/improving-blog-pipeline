### WARN Items (Recommended Fixes)

**1. Checkpoint 2A — Stat Needs Source Follow-Up**

> "64% of such organizations experience this, averaging approximately 232 corporate data exposure incidents per day through personal accounts [NEEDS SOURCE: confirm report name, author, and publish date before publishing.]"

Tag is correctly applied and noted in the draft. This is the article's strongest urgency argument. Confirm the source with Robin before or shortly after publication — do not let this run long as a perpetual flag.

**2. Checkpoint 3E — Slightly Preachy Closing**

> "Leaders who do not help their people develop AI capability are not protecting them. They are positioning them to be replaced by someone outside the organization who already has it."

Frames the point as a judgment on leaders rather than a stated outcome. Consider: "The practical gap: an employee replaced by someone outside the organization who already has this capability is an outcome that plays out before anyone notices the risk." Same point, stated as observation rather than verdict.

**3. Checkpoint 4C — SharePoint War Story Has No Narrative Arc**

The SharePoint section states the risk and implication cleanly but reads as a technical briefing, not a war story. Add one sentence of what Robin or the team actually encountered: what they expected, what surprised them, what decision it forced. The facts are present; the tension is not.

**4. Checkpoint 4D — Post-Placeholder Restatement**

> "Every agent has one measurable output. Every agent receives its input from the prior agent's output file. Describing a goal state guarantees failure. Each agent needs a specific transformation defined: one input, one output."

This paragraph restates what the preceding section just established. If the diagram gets created, this can likely be removed entirely or condensed to a single line callout underneath the diagram. If the diagram is cut, consolidate this with the paragraph above it.

**5. Checkpoint 4E — Vague Success Rate**

> "Success rate, measured across test scenarios, was low."

"Low" is not a number. If Robin stated a rough figure in the transcript (e.g., "it was failing more than half the time"), use it. If not, reframe as a characterization: "It kept failing. Robin couldn't get the workflow to complete reliably as a single agent."

**6. Checkpoint 5B — Section Opener Buries the Answer**

The section "The one reason your agent pipeline will fail before it starts" opens with Robin's story rather than a direct statement of the reason. For snippet and AI citation purposes, consider a single declarative sentence at the top before the story: "The reason is scope. Asking one agent to own a multi-step workflow almost always fails." Then lead into the story.

**7. Checkpoint 6A — Technical Oversimplification**

> "Above roughly the 70% confidence threshold, models are deliberately engineered to randomize, to appear creative and human-like."

Temperature and top-p sampling work differently than a fixed 70% threshold, and "deliberately engineered to randomize" may confuse practitioners who work with these parameters. This is Robin's framing, so preserve his intent, but soften the specificity: "Above a certain confidence threshold, models are engineered to introduce variation rather than always selecting the highest-probability token." Flag for Robin to confirm he's comfortable with however it's worded.
