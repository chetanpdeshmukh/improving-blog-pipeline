### FAIL Items (Fix Before Publishing)

**1. Checkpoint 3B — Banned word: "journey"**

Location: Final section, first paragraph — `"where they are in this journey," Larson says.`

Problem: "journey" is a banned vague noun. Because it appears in a direct quote, it cannot be edited in place without altering attribution.

Fix direction: Paraphrase the quote instead of quoting directly. The meaning survives intact: `Larson says most organizations are "maybe overly generous in their self-assessment of how far along they actually are."` Verify the paraphrase is acceptable to Larson before publishing.

---

**2. Checkpoint 3C — Mirror structure in failure modes section**

Location: "The failure modes AI optimism skips over" — all three bold-header subsections.

Problem: Each subsection follows the same pattern: bold headline → Larson attribution or quote → specific consequence scenario → architectural prescription. The parallel is mechanical enough to register as AI-generated structure.

Fix direction: Vary at least one subsection's construction. For example, lead the third subsection ("Agentic AI makes systems easier to build") with the consequence before introducing Larson's quote, or collapse it into the bridge paragraph below rather than giving it its own bold header. The content is strong; the pattern is the only issue.

### WARN Items (Recommended Fixes)

**1. Checkpoint 2A / 6A — Unattributed and unqualified technical claim**

Location: "Your data is the only moat" section — `"Open-source models are within a year of frontier capability."`

Problem: Stated as fact with no attribution to Larson and no source. This is also a time-sensitive claim that will age quickly. Appears in both the source integrity and technical accuracy checks.

Fix direction: Add `"Larson argues"` or frame it as his read on the trajectory, or verify and hyperlink an authoritative industry source. Do not leave it as a bare assertion.

---

**2. Checkpoint 4C — Thin war story arcs**

Location: New analyst test story (Section 2) and medical claims scenario (Section 2, paragraph 4).

Problem: The analyst test story surfaces a problem ("external customers" had no accounts) but has no outcome — what happened after the gap was discovered? What decision did it force? The medical claims scenario is written as a hypothetical construct ("a claim submitted without a state label may be adjudicated") rather than a documented engagement, which undercuts its credibility.

Fix direction: For the analyst test, add one sentence on what the discovery forced: did it delay the AI deployment? Require a schema remediation sprint? For the medical claims scenario, either confirm it's from a real engagement and frame it as such, or make the hypothetical framing explicit ("as an illustrative example of this risk") so readers don't mistake it for a case study.

---

**3. Checkpoint 5A — Insufficient question-format H2s**

Location: Section headers throughout.

Problem: Only `"How to tell if your data is ready: the new analyst test"` is in question format. The other five H2s are statements or descriptive labels.

Fix direction: Reframe 1–2 additional H2s as questions that match search queries. Candidates: `"What does AI-ready data actually require?"` (replacing current Section 1 header) or `"When is your organization ready to deploy agents?"` (replacing or supplementing the final section).
