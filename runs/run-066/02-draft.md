# Stop Paying for Nearshore Seats. Start Paying for Nearshore Outcomes.

David Sandoval, who has run Improving Mexico's engineering practice for fifteen years, is tired of one conversation: the cost-per-hour negotiation. "If you're only looking at how much is it going to cost me per hour," Sandoval says, "that's probably not going to be the best play for a pure nearshore team." The argument used to be theoretical. AI made it a live experiment — and the results arrive unevenly, depending on whether organizations treated AI adoption as a licensing event or an operating model transformation.

## The Old Nearshore Promise Was Always About Cost. AI Just Made That Obvious.

The labor arbitrage pitch worked for twenty years: hire at a fraction of San Francisco rates and pocket the difference. Mexico produces roughly 130,000 STEM graduates annually [NEEDS SOURCE: confirm whether this is Sandoval's own estimate or a cited figure], a talent pool most U.S. enterprises evaluated solely on time zone and price. Capability rarely entered the negotiation.

AI collapsed that framing. When a single engineer with the right workflow can produce at two to three times prior velocity, cost-per-hour becomes a lagging indicator of almost nothing useful. The metric that matters now is cost-per-outcome: what did this team ship, in what timeframe, with what quality signal?

**When this works:** Teams with clear outcome definitions and delivery metrics find this transition natural — they're already measuring the right thing, and AI changes the numerator.

**When it fails:** Organizations without mature sprint metrics or DORA baselines will find "cost-per-outcome" abstract. Skip that baseline work and AI productivity gains become invisible — exactly when executive confidence in the engagement erodes.

## AI Is a Mirror — It Amplifies Whatever Your Team Already Is

Sandoval frames the dynamic precisely: "AI is going to sort of serve as a mirror to whatever already is happening in the culture of the team and their activities." A team with clear process, good review habits, and collaborative trust will see AI compound their output. A team running on ambiguous requirements and low accountability will see AI accelerate those failure modes.

The sequencing mistake most organizations make is treating AI as the intervention. The engineering framework layer — operating model, role clarity, output review practices, prompt governance — has to exist before the tools are introduced. Distribute licenses into a team without that foundation and the budget disappears quickly, with no clear accounting for what it produced. Sandoval calls one version of this the "Candy Crush competitor" pattern: AI spend at scale, zero business alignment, no accountability for outcomes.

**When this works:** Teams with an existing engineering culture and documented workflow practices can layer AI tooling on top and see measurable velocity gains within a sprint cycle.

**When it fails:** Licenses land on a team without prompt governance or output review protocols, and unreviewed AI-generated code enters production. The failure surface grows with adoption speed.

## What Actually Happened When One Nearshore Team Went All-In on AI

A pharma logistics company in Dallas engaged Improving Mexico across multiple teams. One team was selected for full AI integration — task understanding, releases, monitoring, quality testing, verification — while the others continued as-is. The client's intent was a controlled comparison: measure the AI-enabled team against the rest of the engagement as baseline.

The pressure was an executive mandate to quantify results. The selected team brought what Sandoval describes as "the will to try and experiment and challenge assumptions" — a prerequisite the team had to supply; the AI engineering framework was what Improving Mexico provided.

The team built a CLI AI agent integrated directly into their support ticketing system. The agent read incoming tickets, surfaced contextual history, generated action items, and auto-drafted stakeholder emails. Ticket resolution time dropped from 40 minutes to 4 minutes. Some team members saw 50-60% productivity gains across their broader workload [NEEDS SOURCE: confirm measurement basis with Sandoval — story points per sprint, DORA metrics, or another tracking method]. Results were uneven across individuals, which Sandoval presents as the honest story of AI adoption in a real team.

**When this works:** Teams with psychological safety treat the experiment as a learning loop and surface what's working and what's failing in near-real time.

**When it fails:** Teams that experience a pilot as a performance evaluation game individual metrics instead of improving team workflow — results look good on a dashboard while the operating model stays unchanged.

## The Risks Nobody Puts in the Brochure

Uneven adoption within a single team is the most underrated risk Sandoval names. One engineer goes deep on AI; teammates continue their prior workflow and skip reviewing AI-generated outputs. The security exposure from unreviewed code entering production scales with how aggressively the outlier is producing. Sandoval's teams have seen this pattern introduce vulnerabilities that a standard peer review would have caught.

The distributed hiring problem compounded this. During a video interview, a candidate appeared to answer questions live. The interviewing team noticed the lip sync: someone off-screen was providing the answers in real time. AI lowered the barrier for this kind of deception. Improving Mexico's response was to resequence hiring: cultural alignment and growth mindset come first, foundational engineering competency second, AI proficiency third. Engineers who can perform without AI available are engineers who can evaluate what AI produces.

Tools like Ropes AI — which monitors screen capture and background processes during assessments — add a detection layer to the hiring process. Human judgment and engineering fundamentals remain the primary screen. A hiring process built on detection tooling alone will eventually surface teams built on undiscovered gaps.

**When this works:** Team-wide AI adoption programs with explicit output review checkpoints catch errors before they compound and surface the engineers who need additional support.

**When it fails:** AI adoption treated as an individual initiative, without team-level review, creates a two-tier team — high-speed producers and reviewers unprepared to evaluate what they're approving.

## The Pendulum: Where Should Your Organization Actually Sit?

Sandoval uses a pendulum to frame the executive decision. The left anchor: full headcount, no AI, leaving velocity and capability gains unrealized. The right anchor: full automation, minimal team, trading culture and alignment for throughput. Both positions carry real costs, and the choice requires deliberate calibration.

The middle position is an AI-enabled operating model built on nearshore capability. A well-run nearshore team with a strong AI operating model captures the AI productivity gains and the cultural, timezone, and collaboration advantages of nearshore delivery simultaneously.

**When this works:** Organizations that define the thinking-to-execution ratio in their workflow can locate themselves on this spectrum deliberately — automating task compression while preserving judgment-intensive collaboration in the human layer.

**When it fails:** Organizations that skip the operating model work and jump to positioning end up holding a pendulum with no framework underneath it. The swing becomes unpredictable.

The positioning question Sandoval leaves with executives: where in your workflow does judgment need to happen, and where is explicit task definition sufficient? The answers determine where your team should operate, geographically and operationally.

## What to Look for in an AI-Ready Nearshore Partner

Ask one question before anything else: show me your AI engineering framework. Ask for the actual operating model with specifics — prompt governance, token spend observability, output review before code ships. A partner still in the license-distribution phase will answer in generalities. A partner ahead of that curve has already run internal experiments and can show you results.

The upskilling question matters equally. AI capability in engineering teams requires a structured program. A nearshore partner who built that program before you engaged them brings the learning curve advantage to the engagement. A partner building it alongside you is charging you for their own education.

**When this works:** A partner with documented AI governance and observable outcomes from internal pilots can calibrate their operating model to your delivery context.

**When it fails:** A partner whose AI strategy exists only on slide decks means inheriting their experiment as your production risk.

## Will AI Eventually Make Nearshore Irrelevant?

The credible objection: if AI can compress tasks, summarize meetings, translate documents, and generate code at scale, why does a distributed team add value? Sandoval's answer is that AI moves collaboration higher in the value chain. As tasks get compressed, the remaining work requires more judgment, more alignment, and more cultural trust — exactly what a well-run nearshore team provides.

The "context tax" and "culture tax" grow invisibly as organizations reduce the human layer. The costs accumulate until alignment collapses, usually during a critical delivery window when there's no time to rebuild what was dismantled. Sandoval notes AI gains are currently uneven — that honest acknowledgment matters before making structural decisions.

**When this works:** Organizations that engage this question honestly revise their partner selection criteria and build AI adoption standards into contract expectations from the start.

**When it fails:** Organizations that use the question as permission to defer structural decisions end up behind the organizations that acted when the window was open.

AI has already reshaped nearshore delivery. The question executives face now is whether their current nearshore partner is the kind of team that reshaping revealed as worth keeping.
